TEAM is a live pump.fun scanner with a market maker attached. Every minute it reads every runner on Jupiter's boards, finds every coin riding that runner's name, and measures where each one sits against it. When you pair a main with its betas, one maker wallet watches them and buys the betas that fall behind the main and trims the ones that run ahead. Below is exactly how every part works, what it costs, who holds what, and where every number comes from.

01Overview

On pump.fun, every coin that runs pulls a crowd behind it: same-ticker copies, spin-offs, "baby" and "king" versions, fresh launches named after it within minutes. These are the betas. The coin they ride is the main. Betas move when the main moves, but not evenly: some lag, some overshoot, some never catch up.

TEAM does two things with that. First, the scanner shows you, every minute, every pump.fun runner and the whole crowd riding it, with each beta's live gap to its main. Second, the maker: you pick one main and up to five betas, fund one fresh wallet with SOL, and the engine trades the betas against the main inside rules you set. The main is only the signal. The maker never buys or sells it.

  the main ──▶ its move since kickoff ──┐
                                         ├──▶ the gap ──▶ behind a band?  buy that beta
  each beta ─▶ its move since kickoff ──┘    per beta     ahead a band?   trim that beta
                                                           inside?         do nothing

TEAM is independent. It is not affiliated with pump.fun, Jupiter, Dexscreener, GeckoTerminal or any coin it lists.

02The scanner

A scan runs at most once a minute and is shared by everyone looking at the page. Each scan does four things:

  1. Reads the boards. Jupiter's trending, most-traded and organic-score boards for the last hour, plus trending over five minutes. Only pump.fun launches are kept (the launchpad field, or a mint ending in pump), and only those with at least $100K market cap and $10K liquidity. The 20 that appear on the most boards, then by hourly volume, become this scan's mains.
  2. Finds each main's crowd. TEAM builds search keys from the main's ticker and the distinctive words of its name (common words like "coin", "inu" or "baby" are ignored), then searches Dexscreener and Jupiter for every Solana coin carrying them, and checks every pump.fun launch it read from Solana in the last 24 hours.
  3. Measures every beta. For each beta, the gap to its main over 5 minutes, 1 hour and 24 hours: ln((1 + beta move) / (1 + main move)). A beta more than one default band (10%) behind over the last hour is marked behind; more than a band ahead, ahead; otherwise in step.
  4. Compares with the last scan. New mains, betas that just appeared, and betas that just crossed the band show up in the "this scan" feed.

A beta is tradeable when it has at least $3,000 of liquidity. Betas bigger than their main are dropped (they are a different story, not a beta). "Team them" pre-picks the five deepest tradeable betas; open the row to change the picks.

The scanner's gap uses the last hour because that's what every coin has in common. A live team measures from its own kickoff instead, so its gaps start at zero the moment it goes live.

03Betas and follow

The deep scan (the Betas section) goes further for one main. It searches Jupiter, Dexscreener and three days of on-chain launches, keeps up to 40 betas, and for the seven deepest tradeable ones pulls real 15-minute candles from GeckoTerminal for both the beta and the main.

Follow is the correlation of their 15-minute log returns over the same candles, from −100% to 100%. A high follow means that when the main moves, the beta moves the same way in the same window. That's the property the maker relies on: a beta with a high follow that has fallen behind is more likely to be pulled back up when the main runs again. A beta with a follow near zero is just a coin with a similar name.

  • same ticker: the beta uses exactly the main's ticker. Often a copy, sometimes the original on another pool.
  • fresh · on chain: only Solana knows about it yet. TEAM read its creation straight from pump.fun's program logs.
  • launched: how long after the main the beta was created.

04The lab

The lab puts the main and its betas on the same real candles (5m, 15m or 1h), rebased to 100, and draws each beta's gap to the main underneath with your band shaded. Hover anywhere to read every line at that moment.

Move the band, buy and trim sliders and the lab replays the rule over those exact candles: start with 100 units of cash, buy a beta when it falls a new band behind, trim when it runs a new band ahead, at most two trades per candle, with a 0.5% cost on every trade. It reports the trades it would have made, where the maker would have ended against plain cash, and what simply holding the betas did.

A replay of the past, not a promise. Real fills differ from candle closes, and liquidity changes.

05Pairing a team

  1. Team. The main and one to 5 betas. Each beta must have at least $3,000 of liquidity when you create the team.
  2. Rules. Band, buy share, trim share, cooldown between passes, the most SOL per trade, and the highest price impact a swap may have. The lab replay updates as you move them.
  3. Fund. Sign in with a free message signature, then TEAM creates a brand-new maker wallet for this team only, and you send it SOL from your wallet (at least 0.05 SOL). You sign that transfer yourself.

When the deposit lands, the team goes live and records its kickoff: the main's price and every beta's price at that moment. Every gap from then on is measured from kickoff. You can reset kickoff to today's prices at any time.

06The engine

Every few minutes (your cooldown, default 3 minutes), the engine runs one pass for each live team:

  1. Read the main's and every beta's live price from Jupiter.
  2. For each beta, the gap since kickoff: gap = ln((beta / beta₀) / (main / main₀)), and its level: floor(|gap| / band).
  3. Each beta keeps a ladder: the deepest level it has already been traded at, on that side. The maker trades only when a beta reaches a new level. Back inside the band, the ladder resets. If the gap flips sides, the ladder starts again on the new side.
  4. Behind (gap below −band at a new level): buy that beta with your buy share of free SOL, never more than your max per trade, and never more than 5 SOL of the wallet in total, keeping 0.015 SOL back for fees and rent.
  5. Ahead (gap above band at a new level): sell your trim share of that beta's bag.
  6. At most two trades per pass, starting with the beta furthest from the main. Every swap is quoted by Jupiter first and skipped (and logged) if its price impact is above your limit.
gap  +30% ┤                    ●  trim (new level 3)
     +20% ┤               ●       trim (new level 2)
     +10% ┤──────────●─────────── band
        0 ┼── kickoff ───────────────────────────────
     -10% ┤───────────────────────── band ──●──────── buy (new level 1)
     -20% ┤                                     ●     buy (new level 2)

Passes are driven by a scheduler that calls the engine every 30 seconds; each team runs only when its own cooldown is due. Every trade, skip and change is written to the team's public log with its transaction.

07Custody and control

The maker has to trade while you sleep, so the server holds the maker's key. It is generated on the server for one team only, encrypted at rest with AES-256-GCM under a secret the operator sets, and never shown to anyone. The maker only ever holds the SOL you send it and the betas it buys.

  • Only the owner's wallet can change the rules, pause, reset kickoff, add SOL or withdraw. You prove it with a free message signature; nothing is ever signed on your behalf from your own wallet.
  • Withdraw sells every beta, one per pass, then closes the empty token accounts (their rent comes back) and sends every remaining lamport to the owner's wallet, minus the network fee. Then the team is closed.
  • Every maker is public. Its address, balances, trades and log are on the team page and on Solana.

Fund only what you can afford to lose. Holding a key on a server is a risk in itself, however carefully it is stored.

09Data sources

  • Jupiter: the trending, traded and organic boards (the mains), token search, 5m/1h/24h moves, liquidity, icons, live prices, and every swap quote and transaction the maker sends.
  • Dexscreener: pair search for betas, and the market (price, liquidity, market cap) for any contract you paste.
  • GeckoTerminal: the 5m, 15m and 1h candles behind the lab and the follow score.
  • Solana: every pump.fun launch, read from the program's create events (name, ticker, creator, time) at most every 25 seconds and kept three days; the maker's balances; and every transaction.

When a source doesn't answer, the scan says so on the page instead of filling the gap.

11Risks

  • You can lose money. Buying a beta that's behind is a bet that it catches up. Many betas are behind because they're dying, and meme coins can go to zero.
  • Rugs and thin liquidity. A beta's creator can pull liquidity or dump. Swaps in thin pools move the price against the maker.
  • The main can turn. When the main dumps, betas that were "behind" can stay behind for good.
  • Server custody. The maker's key lives on the server so it can trade unattended.
  • Not advice. Nothing here is financial advice. The scanner shows what's on chain; what you do with it is your call.
